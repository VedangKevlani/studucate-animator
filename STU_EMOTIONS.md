# Stu's emotions

What Stu can express, what he does, and what to write in a script to trigger
it. Every example line below has been checked against the mood reader.

## How it works

- Stu reads the script **one sentence at a time**. Each sentence gets one
  emotion, and he switches when he reaches it.
- Sentences end at `.` `!` `?` or `...`, so `Naaah... Don't worry.` is two
  sentences: a shrug, then reassurance.
- **Phrases beat single words.** "Never think of yourself as a burden" reads
  as reassuring, even though "burden" on its own reads as sad.
- **Punctuation counts.** A question with no other clue reads as curious. `!`
  makes him bigger. A lone `Okay?` at the end is a reassuring check-in.
- **Emphasis makes it stronger:** `!!!`, a word in CAPS, or a stretched word
  ("sooo", "naaah"). A very strong "happy" becomes "excited".
- A sentence with no clue at all is **neutral**. He still talks, breathes,
  blinks and makes small arm movements.

## The emotions

Each emotion changes his **face** (eyebrows, eyelids, and his mouth between
words) and his **arms, legs and body**. While he talks, his mouth lip-syncs,
his brows lift a little on emphasis and his knees give a small bounce.

| Emotion | Face | Arms, legs and body | Say something like | Trigger words and phrases |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Saying hi** | Brows raised, friendly eyes | Waves one hand high, weight on one leg | "Hi, I'm Stu!" · "Good morning, welcome back." | hi, hey, hello, welcome, howdy, bye, goodbye (at the **start** of a sentence) · good morning, good afternoon, good evening, nice to meet, see you, welcome back |
| **Happy** | Brows up, smiling eyes | Small hop with feet tucked, arms slightly out | "That is a great answer!" · "Thanks for sharing that with me." | glad, happy, great, good, nice, love, awesome, amazing, wonderful, fantastic, yay, fun, enjoy, smile, excellent, perfect, cool, proud, congrats, thanks, haha, lol, beautiful, brilliant, sweet |
| **Excited** | Brows high | Crouches, jumps with feet tucked, both hands up | "Let's get started!" · "Woohoo, you did it!!!" | excited, thrilled, woohoo, yippee, incredible, epic, ready · let's go, let's get started, can't wait, so excited, here we go · or any happy line with lots of `!` or CAPS |
| **Reassuring** | Soft, slightly raised brows, warm half-lidded eyes | Opens his arms, leans in, nods | "Don't worry, you've got this." · "Take your time." · "Okay?" | don't worry, no worries, it's okay, it's fine, you're not, here to help, here for you, I'm here, you can do, you got this, take your time, no problem, all good, not your fault, one step at a time, trust me, come to me, let me help, I've got you · always, safe, together, promise, reassure · a lone "Okay?" / "Right?" / "Deal?" |
| **Agreeing** | Brows up a little, relaxed eyes | Nods, arms relaxed | "Yes, exactly." · "That makes sense." | yes, yeah, yep, sure, right, exactly, absolutely, definitely, correct, okay · of course, you're right, that's right, good point, makes sense, sounds good |
| **Disagreeing** | One brow up, one down (skeptical), flat mouth | Shrugs with palms up, head shake, weight on one leg | "Nah, not really." · "I don't think so." | no, nah, nope, wrong, never, disagree · not really, I don't think so, no no, not at all, of course not |
| **Curious** | One brow raised high | Tilts his head, leans in, taps a foot | "What do you think happens next?" · "I wonder why that works." | why, how, what, which, where, when, who (in a **question**) · wonder, curious, interesting · I wonder, tell me, what if, how about, have you ever, do you know · any other question |
| **Thinking it over** | Brows gently raised in the middle, half-closed eyes, flat mouth | Weight on one leg, one knee bent, a slow foot tap | "Hmm... let me think." · "Well, maybe." | hmm, um, uh, well, maybe, perhaps, probably, guess, suppose, actually · let me think, let's see, I guess, I think, kind of, sort of · a sentence trailing off with `...` |
| **Surprised** | Brows shoot up, “O” mouth | Arms fly out, small jump with feet tucked | "Whoa!" · "Really?!" | wow, whoa, omg, oh, really, unbelievable, gosh, huh · no way, oh my, oh wow, I can't believe, who knew · `?!` |
| **Sad** | Worried brows, droopy eyes, flat mouth | Head droops, knees bend, arms hang, a sigh | "I'm sorry, that sounds hard." · "Unfortunately, that answer is wrong." | sad, sorry, unfortunately, miss, lonely, cry, hurt, lost, tired, upset, disappointed, worried, afraid, scared · I'm sorry, I miss, too bad, not good · "not happy", "not good" (a negated happy word) |
| **Frustrated** | Brows pulled down and in (cross), narrowed eyes, flat mouth | Throws his arms down and stomps, small head shake | "Ugh, not again." · "Come on, seriously?" | ugh, argh, annoying, annoyed, hate, angry, mad, frustrating, seriously, ridiculous · come on, not again, give me a break |
| **Neutral** | Relaxed, smiling | No gesture: breathing, blinking, weight shifts, small arm beats while talking | "Photosynthesis turns sunlight into chemical energy." | nothing emotional in the sentence |

### Leg moves

Put one of these words in a sentence and Stu does the move **on top of** that
sentence's emotion: "Let's jump for joy!" is happy *and* a jump. The first leg
word in a sentence wins. Everyday phrases don't trigger them ("one step at a
time" stays reassuring, "I can't wait" stays excited).

| Leg move | What Stu does | Say something like | Trigger words |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jump** | Star jump: deep crouch, springs into the air with legs out and arms up, lands deep | "Let's jump for joy!" · "Hop on over here." | jump, jumping, hop, hopping, leap |
| **Dance** | Swings his weight side to side, kicking the free leg out each time, arms swinging | "Time to dance!" · "Let's celebrate your score." | dance, dancing, party, groove, boogie, celebrate |
| **March** | High knees one after the other, body bobbing, big arm swings | "Let's march to the next lesson." · "Walk with me." | march, marching, walk, walking, stroll, parade |
| **Stomp** | Swings a leg up and out, then slams it down with his whole body dropping, twice | "Ugh, I could stomp my feet!" | stomp, stomps, stomping |
| **Tap** | Leans onto one leg and taps the other foot out to the side | "I am tapping my foot here." · "Are you getting impatient?" | tap, tapping, impatient, tick |
| **Bounce** | Deep knee bounces, feet leaving the floor on the way up | "Bounce with me!" · "That's a bouncy one." | bounce, bouncing, bouncy, wiggle |
| **Kick** | Leans away and kicks one leg high out to the side | "Kick the ball into the goal!" | kick, kicking, goal, soccer, football |

His legs are lively without trigger words too: every emotion moves them, his
knees dip on each talking beat (more for happy and excited lines), and when
idle he taps a foot, shifts his weight or lifts a heel every few seconds.

### Sound tags

Tags are their own moment in the clip, and Stu reacts to them too:

| Tag | Stu shows |
|---|---|
| `[laugh]` `[chuckle]` | Happy |
| `[sigh]` `[sniff]` | Sad |
| `[gasp]` | Surprised |
| `[groan]` | Frustrated |
| `[clear throat]` | Thinking it over |
| `[shush]` | Disagreeing |
| `[cough]` | Neutral |

`[pause]` is **not** a real tag (Stu reads it out as the word "pause"), and
`*asterisks*` aren't supported either. See CLAUDE.md.

### While generating

While a new clip is being made, Stu shows his **thinking** pose: hand on
chin, worried brows. It's the only pose where his face changes, because it's
a separate drawing.

## Script-writing tips

- **One feeling per sentence.** If a sentence mixes two ("Great job, but I'm
  worried"), the stronger clue wins. Split it to get both.
- **Lead with the trigger.** Greetings only count at the start of a sentence.
- **Use punctuation on purpose.** `!` for energy, `?` for curiosity, `...`
  for a thoughtful trail-off, `?!` for surprise.
- **Big moves are short.** The wave, cheer, shrug and jump play once when the
  sentence starts, then he settles into that emotion's posture.

## Adding to the list

**New trigger words or phrases for an existing emotion:** add them to
`MOOD_WORDS` or `MOOD_PHRASES` in [static/mood.js](static/mood.js), in
lowercase. Phrases count double, so prefer a phrase when a single word is
ambiguous. Then add an example to the table above.

**New leg-move words** go in `MOOD_ACTIONS` in [static/mood.js](static/mood.js).
A brand-new leg move also needs keyframes in `ACTIONS` in
[static/stu-avatar/stu-avatar.js](static/stu-avatar/stu-avatar.js) (same format
as `GESTURES`).

**A new emotion** (for example "proud" or "confused") needs three things:

1. **Triggers:** a new key in `MOOD_WORDS` / `MOOD_PHRASES` in
   [static/mood.js](static/mood.js).
2. **Body language:** an entry in `MOODS` in
   [static/stu-avatar/stu-avatar.js](static/stu-avatar/stu-avatar.js) (the
   held posture: arm angles, head tilt, lean, crouch). Optionally add a
   one-shot move in `GESTURES`, written as keyframes. Arm angles run from 0
   (hanging) to 81 (straight out) to 135 (the highest they go).
3. **Label:** an entry in `MOOD_LABELS` in [static/app.js](static/app.js).
   That's the word shown under Stu.

An emotion's **face** is part of its `MOODS` entry too: `browL`/`browR` move
each brow (px, negative = up), `tiltL`/`tiltR` tilt it (degrees, positive =
worried, negative = cross), `lids` lowers the eyelids (0 to 0.65), and
`mouth` picks his mouth between words (`rest` smile, `flat`, `round`).
**Legs** use `crouch` (knee bend), `hipX` (weight shift) and `liftL`/`liftR`
(raise a foot), in the held posture or in a `GESTURES` keyframe.
